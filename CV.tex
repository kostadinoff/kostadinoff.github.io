\section{Academic profile}\label{academic-profile}

Physician-epidemiologist and Chief Assistant Professor in the Department
of Social Medicine and Public Health at the Medical University of
Plovdiv, working at the intersection of epidemiology, health economics
and health policy. Research centres on how health systems allocate
scarce resources --- orphan medicines and rare cancers, the cost of
illness, health technology assessment, and the health consequences of
the urban environment --- using routinely collected administrative
health data and real-world evidence.

Holds an MD, a PhD in social medicine and public health, an MPH, and
master's degrees in economics and finance, statistical business
consulting, and impact assessment and policy appraisal, and is currently
reading for a Master of Laws. Board-certified in Social Medicine and
Organisation of Health Care and Pharmacy, and currently in residency
training in Infectious Disease Epidemiology.

Develops and publishes open research software:
\href{kkstatfun.qmd}{\texttt{kkstatfun}}, an R toolkit of 146 functions
for medical statistics, epidemiology and health economics. Associate
Editor at JMIR Publications and founder of the Social Epidemiology Lab.

\section{Research interests}\label{research-interests}

Epidemiology · Social medicine and public health · Infectious disease
epidemiology · Health policy and health technology assessment ·
Real-world evidence and health economics · Environmental and urban
health · Biostatistics and reproducible research

The publication record is organised around six research programmes:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Rare diseases, rare cancers and access to orphan medicines.}
  Policy analysis of definitions, registration, organisation of care and
  reimbursement; timeliness of market access; national and EU-level
  policy alternatives.
\item
  \textbf{Health economics, HTA and real-world evidence.}
  Cost-of-illness studies (myasthenia gravis, spinal muscular atrophy,
  osteoporosis, end-stage kidney disease, terminal cancer),
  budget-impact validation, and public expenditure analysis from a payer
  perspective.
\item
  \textbf{Infectious disease epidemiology and antimicrobial resistance.}
  COVID-19 epidemiology and vaccine impact, urinary tract and sexually
  transmitted infections, antimicrobial resistance trends,
  echinococcosis, and HPV-attributable expenditure.
\item
  \textbf{Environmental and urban health.} Air pollution and
  cardiovascular risk, green space and land cover, health impact
  assessment in spatial planning, and stakeholder evidence for urban
  environmental policy.
\item
  \textbf{Health professions education and digital health.}
  Virtual-reality and simulation-based training, trauma-informed care,
  and mental health literacy among school and health professionals.
\item
  \textbf{Applied biostatistics, prediction and reproducible research.}
  External validation of clinical prediction and AI tools, diagnostic
  accuracy, and open, reproducible statistical software.
\end{enumerate}
